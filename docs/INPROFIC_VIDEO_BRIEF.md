# INPROFIC — Video & Jingle Brief

*A short, non-technical overview for scripting a demo video or jingle. Everything here describes what the software does today.*

## The one-line story
**INPROFIC runs the whole journey from "we bought flour" to "the customer got their order and we know exactly what we earned."**

## Who it is for
People who *make or prepare what they sell* — bakeries, restaurants, small manufacturers — and also shops and wholesalers who simply buy and resell. The words on screen change to fit the business: a bakery talks about *bake*, a restaurant about *prepare*, a factory about *manufacture*.

## The problem (the opening scene)
Notebook for stock. WhatsApp for orders. A spreadsheet for costs. A separate app for deliveries. At month-end nobody can say why profit looks the way it does.

## The promise
One connected system where every step feeds the next, so the business can *explain* its numbers, not just record them.

## The journey, in six beats
1. **Buy** — raise a purchase, receive the goods. Stock and supplier balances update by themselves, and the price paid is remembered forever.
2. **Make** — pick a product, say how much; the system works out the materials, releases them, and records what was really made, what was wasted and what it cost.
3. **Sell anywhere** — a branded online store, shareable order links, a counter POS, or your own website. Customers pay first, so unpaid baskets never disturb stock.
4. **Deliver** — your own riders with their own simple phone screen, delivery zones drawn on a map, fees from real distance, and a clear tracking timeline. Or connect an outside courier partner.
5. **Get paid** — gateway payments or plain bank transfer with verification. Money owed is never confused with money received.
6. **Know** — profit, what customers owe you, what you owe suppliers, and an audit trail nobody can quietly rewrite.

## Where INPROFIC goes further (the "wow" moments)
- **From ingredient to profit.** Costs flow from purchase to production to sale, so profit is real, not guessed.
- **The kitchen uses the same order form, trimmed to what it needs.** Bakers, chefs or production operators (named to fit the business) log what they made: no prices, no customers, no payments. They can adjust the flexible material from the order page. The order waits with no channel, and a manager later assigns each portion to the channel it belongs to. A *Guided* version has no access to recipes at all.
- **Flexible materials.** Yeast, seasoning, a pinch more — decided at the bench, recorded honestly, costed correctly.
- **Shared production runs.** Make five customers' orders in one batch while keeping each order separate.
- **Social selling built in.** Generate QR codes aimed at specific platforms or campaigns, so each audience lands on the right order page.
- **Delivery that respects the customer.** The fee and method are shown *before* payment, and a higher charge is never added afterwards.
- **Built-in trust.** External auditors get a read-only workspace where they can raise questions without changing a single record.
- **Roles that fit the job.** A rider sees only their deliveries; a cashier lands straight in the POS; a baker sees only what they need.

## Pricing, in plain words
Start with a free trial. Pay monthly, or quarterly, bi-annually or yearly for a better rate. A simple switch on the pricing page shows the price for each choice, and a full comparison explains every feature with a small info tip.

## Tone and look
Warm, confident, practical. Real kitchens, counters and riders, not stock-photo offices. Warm reds and golds on screen; show the product in motion, not slides.

## Tagline options
- *From ingredient to profit.*
- *Make it. Sell it. Know it.*
- *One system. Every step.*

## Jingle (original, ~20 seconds, upbeat Afrobeat-pop feel)
> **Buy it, make it, sell it, send it —**
> **INPROFIC helps you track it, mend it, bend it into profit!**
> *(beat drop)*
> **From the flour to the door, from the oven to the score —**
> **One system, every step. INPROFIC!**

**Sound cue ideas:** a till "ching" on "sell it," a scooter horn on "send it," a short shaker roll into the logo.

## What *not* to claim
Do not promise features that are plan-dependent as "included for everyone," and do not show real customer data. Delivery partner brands should only appear once their integration is switched on for the business.
